## Problem

Road and gravel forks have to be laterally stiff for precise steering, yet compliant in the
fore-aft direction to absorb road vibration. BMC's Hinge Fork addresses this with a deliberate
flex zone near the dropouts. The goal of this project was to understand _how_ that design works
and to quantify its trade-offs.

## Approach

- **Reverse-engineered geometry** from product photos, published dimensions and measurements,
  modelled parametrically in SolidWorks.
- **Load cases** derived from ISO 4210 (frontal impact, braking, fatigue under alternating
  fore-aft load).
- **FE model** of the fork in ANSYS with a simplified layup represented as orthotropic shell
  properties; mesh convergence checked in the hinge region.
- **Comparison** against a rigid reference fork of equal mass to isolate the effect of the hinge.

## Result

The hinge geometry increases fore-aft compliance by roughly **18 %** while lateral stiffness drops
by less than **5 %** — a favourable trade-off for comfort without vague steering. The lowest fatigue
safety factor (**1.9**) occurs at the transition between hinge and dropout, which matches where the
manufacturer appears to have added material.

**Limitations:** the composite layup is unknown, so absolute values are estimates; the relative
comparison is the meaningful result.

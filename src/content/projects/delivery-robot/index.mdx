## Problem

The ETH Innovationsprojekt asked teams to build an autonomous robot that follows a marked track,
stops at delivery stations and hands over a small payload — within a fixed budget, a 10-week
timeline and with parts from the course kit. The evaluation rewarded speed, reliability over
repeated runs, and the precision of the delivery itself.

Our main constraints:

- **Budget:** CHF 150 for everything not in the kit {/* TODO */}
- **Size:** footprint of max. 250 × 250 mm
- **Reliability:** at least 45 of 50 runs without manual intervention

## Approach

**Drive and sensing.** A differential drive with two geared DC motors and an array of five
reflective IR sensors. The line position is estimated as a weighted average of the sensor
readings and fed into a PID controller running at 500 Hz on the Arduino.

**Custom motor shield.** Instead of stacking off-the-shelf boards, I sketched a single motor shield
(dual H-bridge, current sensing, sensor headers, servo outputs) in KiCad. It reduced wiring
errors and halved the stack height.

**Spring-loaded shooter.** Payloads are launched by a compression spring that is tensioned by a
servo-driven cam and released by a latch. Launch distance is set by the cam angle, calibrated
against measured landing positions.

**Benchmark tests.** We defined a fixed test protocol (50 runs, three track layouts, logged lap
times and failures) and used it after every significant change, so design decisions were driven
by data rather than by single impressive runs.

**Web interface.** The robot streams telemetry over serial to a small web app (Web Serial API),
which shows sensor values and PID terms live and allows tuning gains without re-flashing.

## Result

The robot completed **48 of 50** benchmark runs autonomously, with a mean track speed of
**0.42 m/s** and a shooter landing accuracy of **±12 mm** at 1 m distance. Live tuning via the
web interface cut our PID tuning time from hours to minutes.

**What I would do differently:** characterise the spring and cam earlier — the shooter needed two
redesigns because the first spring constant estimate was off by about 30 %.
